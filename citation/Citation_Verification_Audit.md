## Fresh primary-source checks (30 October 2026)

The checks below independently resolve several high-priority entries that the workbook had left as candidates or marked for verification. These checks confirm bibliographic records and abstract-level scope; they do not establish that every sentence, figure, or page number in the manuscript is supported.

- **N01 — Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?** The arXiv record is real and the work appears in the NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track. The peer-reviewed version describes MAST-Data with 1,600+ annotated traces from seven multi-agent frameworks and identifies 14 failure modes across system design, inter-agent misalignment, and task verification. This is directly relevant to multi-agent failure analysis. Cite it for the study's taxonomy/findings, not as proof that every agentic research workflow exhibits the same failure distribution. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) · [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)
- **N02 — Where LLM Agents Fail and How They Can Learn From Failures.** The arXiv record and abstract are confirmed. The abstract describes cascading failures, AgentErrorTaxonomy, AgentErrorBench, and AgentDebug. No peer-reviewed venue was confirmed in this check; cite as an arXiv preprint unless independently updated. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370)
- **N04 — Which Agent Causes Task Failures and When?** The arXiv record and ICML 2025/PMLR publication are confirmed. The abstract reports better agent-level than step-level attribution (53.5% versus 14.2% for the best method), a useful limitation for causal error tracing. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212) · [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25ab.html)
- **N03, N05, N06, and N07 remain unverified in this pass.** Do not describe them as peer-reviewed or rely on precise figure, venue, or quantitative claims until their primary records and full texts have been checked.

## Interpretation limits and actionable manuscript fixes

- In the original-reference table, **P10's “Not Supported / Misattributed” label refers to the workbook's associated correlated-error claim**, not to whether the AutoGen paper exists. Keep AutoGen for framework description; do not cite it alone as evidence for correlated error propagation.
- **P11 MetaGPT** can support a description of a multi-agent software framework, but its presence does not by itself establish a general causal claim about error propagation.
- **P03 Self-Consistency** should be described as sampling multiple reasoning paths and aggregating/selecting the most consistent answer, not merely adding explicit intermediate reasoning steps.
- **P09 Reflexion** supports iterative verbal feedback and memory-based refinement; the claim that an incorrect critique makes a draft 'more forceful' is an inference and should be labelled as such or removed.
- **P12 Generative Agents** describes memory, reflection, and planning, but does not directly evaluate error propagation through memory. Phrase any connection as a research motivation, not a demonstrated result.
- **P21 citation generation and P22 FActScore** are relevant to evidence/citation quality and factuality evaluation. A claimed lack of workflow-level propagation metrics is the review author's synthesis/gap observation, not a finding established by either paper alone.
- The current repository has a PDF manuscript but no confirmed editable manuscript source in the paper directory. Therefore this pass updates the audit and source catalog, but does not claim to have checked every in-text citation location or modified the PDF's prose. A full sentence-by-sentence claim audit requires the exact final manuscript text/source.
